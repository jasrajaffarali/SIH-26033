## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution

1. Detailed explanation of the proposed solution
We propose a digital marketplace that connects farmers and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) directly with consumers and bulk buyers. Each participating village will have a shared collection and storage point where farmers can store their produce before it is sold.

Farmers or an elected village coordinator will record each product’s type, quantity, quality, harvest date and price in the app. Buyers can browse available products, place orders and choose delivery or collection. The app will show stock availability, produce prices, delivery charges and transport details.

A locally hired delivery person or transport provider will deliver orders. Their fee, along with any storage or handling charge, will be shown separately. The farmer sells the produce directly and receives the agreed sale amount; the coordinator and delivery provider are paid for their services. They do not buy produce for resale.


2. How it addresses the problem
The app removes traders who purchase produce from farmers and resell it at a higher price. Direct listings let farmers and buyers see the same product information and agreed price.

Shared village storage can help farmers keep produce safely while waiting for orders. Visible service charges help buyers understand what they are paying for, while farmers can see their expected payment before accepting an order. Stock updates help prevent orders for unavailable goods.


3. Innovation and uniqueness of the solution
The solution combines village-level shared storage with an online direct-sale marketplace. It gives farmers access to local and bulk buyers without requiring them to manage every order or delivery themselves.

The app can also use demand forecasting to help farmers and FPOs estimate which products buyers may need. Route optimization can group nearby deliveries to reduce transport time and cost. These features support the marketplace while keeping service fees visible and separate from the farmer’s produce price.

## Technical Approach

1. Technologies to be used
- Mobile app: Android app for farmers, coordinators, delivery providers and buyers.
- Web dashboard: For FPOs or administrators to manage users, orders and reports.
- Backend and database: To manage farmer profiles, listings, stock, orders, payments and delivery records.
- Mapping and location services: To display storage points and plan delivery routes.
- Digital payments: To record buyer payments and transfer the farmer’s agreed amount and service fees.
- AI features: Demand forecasting based on past orders and seasonal data; route suggestions based on delivery locations.
- Notifications: SMS or app alerts for new orders, payment updates, low stock and delivery status.

2. Methodology and process for implementation
Proposed order flow:

Farmer adds produce → Coordinator records or verifies quantity and quality → Listing appears in the app → Buyer places an order → App shows produce price and separate service charges → Farmer confirms → Delivery provider collects and delivers the order → Buyer confirms receipt → Payment is recorded and distributed.

For an initial prototype, we will build farmer listings, stock updates, buyer ordering, price breakdowns, delivery details and order status tracking. Demand forecasting and route optimization can be added using sample or pilot data.

## Feasibility and Viability

1. Analysis of the feasibility of the idea
The idea can be piloted in one village by partnering with a farmer group or FPO and using an existing suitable storage location. Farmers can list produce using smartphones, while a coordinator can help those who need assistance.

The app can begin with manual stock verification, basic order management and locally arranged transport. This makes it possible to test the process before investing in advanced AI features or additional storage facilities.


2. Potential challenges and risks
- Produce may spoil or lose quality during storage.
- Stock or quality information may be inaccurate.
- Farmers and buyers may have limited access to smartphones or reliable internet.
- Delivery costs may be high for small or distant orders.
- Payment, cancellations and disputes may create delays.
- Demand can change with seasons and local market conditions.


3. Strategies for overcoming these challenges
- Set clear storage, hygiene and quality-check procedures.
- Record quantities at intake and update stock after each order.
- Provide coordinator-assisted listing and ordering, with SMS or phone support where needed.
- Group nearby orders and use route planning to reduce delivery costs.
- Show order terms, fees and expected payments before confirmation; keep digital payment records.
- Start with a small pilot and use sales data to improve demand forecasts and operating procedures.

## Impact and Benefits

1. Potential impact on the target audience
The marketplace is intended to help farmers and FPOs reach buyers directly, while giving consumers and bulk buyers clearer access to product availability, pricing and delivery information. Village coordinators and delivery providers can earn transparent service fees or salaries for their work.


2. Benefits of the solution
- Economic: Farmers can see the expected sale amount and avoid resale markups by traders. Buyers can see produce prices and service charges separately.
- Social: A village coordinator can support farmers with listings, orders and communication.
- Operational: Shared storage, stock tracking and delivery coordination can reduce avoidable handling and order confusion.
- Environmental: Better demand planning and grouped delivery routes may help reduce unsold produce, spoilage and unnecessary travel.
